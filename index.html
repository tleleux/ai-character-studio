Why the order matters

  • Still first, then animate. Text-to-video re-rolls the face every clip. A trained identity plus a start frame does not.
  • Measure the voice before you render. Words per minute and pause length are checked on the audio before any video credit is spent. Pace is the loudest AI tell in the data; a preset text-to-speech track at 113 words per minute failed three times.
  • Prompt the micro-movements. Relaxed brows, a glance down, one open-palm gesture, room tone at −43 dBFS. Each one is a measured gap between the failed renders and the human baseline.
  • One lock box. The continuity sheet ends in a "do not change" block written as a negative prompt, so every future render carries the same mark, brows, hair and jewelry.

Digital twin differences

Steps 1 to 3 are replaced by your photos: your best photo is the plate and the Element, the training set is your 10 to 15 real shots, and the continuity sheet is a hybrid with your real face pasted into the face panel. Step 6 is your own recording (0 credits) or a clone of it. Everything from step 7 on is identical.

What authority looks like on camera

Experts vs practitioners

  • Experts speak slower: 184 vs 199 WPM.
  • Press lips between phrases (+0.47), furrow more (+0.43), raise brows less (−0.34).
  • Turn the head more, not less: yaw 7.8° vs 5.9°. They also smile more.
  • Cite fewer numbers.
79 clinical vs 141 practitioner reels

Edit and framing targets

  • 5 to 7 cuts per minute, median shot 5 to 6 s.
  • Talking head ≥ 97% of runtime.
  • Face 21 to 24% of frame height, hands in frame 80% of the time.
  • Burned-in text on 55 to 60% of frames; a music bed in about half the reels at −9 dB.
reel forensics · targets

Realism rules for the face

  • Muted catchlights, no oversized specular glare.
  • Visible pores, fine lines, subtle asymmetry, one signature mark.
  • Flat grey plate, no key light, 50 mm. The scene prompt lights the room later.
  • Stills carry most of video quality. A beauty filter is the fastest way to read as 2025 AI.
